Is Morocco running out of rain?

Not exactly. It's getting hotter.

I analysed 34 rainy seasons (1991–2025) across a grid of 62 points covering all of Morocco, then tested which changes are real and which could be chance.

    Finding 03

    The thirsty air.

    Evaporation demand is how much water the air would pull out of soil, plants and reservoirs if it could. Hotter, drier air pulls harder, so the same rain goes less far.

    The proof

    What the data can and can't prove.

    A p-value is the chance of seeing a trend this strong if nothing were really changing. Below 0.05 counts as statistically significant.

    How I built it

    62 points, 34 seasons, every number tested.

    Daily rain and evaporation demand (FAO reference evapotranspiration) from Open-Meteo's historical archive, on a 1° grid (about 100 km) across all of Morocco. Cereal yields from the World Bank. Analysed in Python (pandas, SciPy, GeoPandas) in Google Colab; the site is HTML, CSS and JavaScript with Chart.js. I used AI tools to help write some of the code; I chose the questions, checked the results and wrote the findings.

    Data notes

    • Open-Meteo data is reanalysis: weather observations combined with a model, not readings from one station per city.
    • The grid covers Morocco including its southern provinces. National figures are the average of all 62 points.
    • A rainy season runs September to August, so "2022/23" is matched to the 2023 harvest.
    • Evaporation demand is what the air could take, not water actually lost; when soil is dry, real evaporation is lower.
